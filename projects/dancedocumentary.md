# The Brief
For this project, I filmed and edited a documentary-style interview with a professional dancer, running at least five minutes in length. The work required two distinct filming techniques, at least 50% B-roll footage shot on a tripod, proper lighting, and separate audio recording — all working together to produce something visually and narratively cohesive. Throughout the editing process, I focused on using these technical requirements not just to meet the assignment criteria, but to strengthen the emotional tone of the documentary. The combination of interview footage, performance clips, and carefully framed B-roll helped create a more immersive experience that allowed the audience to connect with both the subject and the cultural significance behind the story.

# The Story Behind the Camera
I interviewed my uncle Ikaika, a kumu hula at an elementary school in Maui and a multiple-time Merrie Monarch Festival performer. Rather than a surface-level profile, the documentary explored the full weight of what he carries — as a dancer, a teacher, and a steward of Hawaiian culture. The footage wove together his words with visuals that reflected the discipline, creativity, and deep cultural responsibility embedded in hula. Rehearsal scenes, performance moments, and quieter behind-the-scenes footage worked together to show that hula is not simply entertainment, but a form of storytelling and cultural preservation passed down through generations. By capturing both the preparation and the performance, the documentary aimed to reveal the dedication and pride that often exist beyond what audiences normally see on stage.

# Reflecting on His Story
This project changed how I think about storytelling. Learning to shape an interview into a cohesive narrative — deciding what to keep, what to cut, and what the footage should say on its own — pushed me in ways a written assignment never could. More than technical skills, I came away with a deeper appreciation for my uncle's work and a clearer sense of how visual media can honor a story rather than just document it. Watching the final documentary come together also made me realize how much meaning can be communicated through pacing, visuals, sound, and emotion rather than dialogue alone. It taught me that documentaries are not only about presenting information, but about creating connection and allowing viewers to experience someone else's perspective in a more personal and impactful way.
